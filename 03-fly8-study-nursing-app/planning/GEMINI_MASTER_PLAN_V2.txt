PART 2. THE ONE RULE THE OWNER CARES ABOUT MOST:
        NURSING INTERVENTIONS ARE NOT THE DOCTOR'S INTERVENTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Every note must keep these three apart, under separate headings. Never mix them.

  MEDICAL MANAGEMENT (the doctor's job; the nurse does NOT do this)
    Diagnosing, prescribing drugs/IV fluids/diet, ordering lab tests and imaging, deciding on
    and performing surgery or invasive procedures (thoracentesis, lumbar puncture, paracentesis,
    central line insertion, intubation decisions). Write it as: "The physician ...".
    The nurse's part in each of these goes under Collaborative interventions.

  INDEPENDENT NURSING INTERVENTIONS (the nurse may do these WITHOUT an order)
    Assessment and monitoring (vital signs, neuro checks, I&O, weight, skin, pain, LOC),
    positioning, airway care within scope, safety (fall, seizure, bleeding, suicide precautions),
    standard precautions and isolation per hospital policy, hygiene and comfort (non-drug),
    nutrition/fluid encouragement within the diet ordered, health teaching, emotional support,
    therapeutic communication, documentation, reporting changes to the physician,
    referral to community programs, home and family care.

  COLLABORATIVE (DEPENDENT) NURSING INTERVENTIONS (need a physician's order or a standing protocol)
    Giving drugs, IV fluids, blood, oxygen therapy, inserting NGT/catheters, collecting ordered
    specimens, preparing the patient for and assisting with procedures, post-procedure care.
    Every line starts with "As ordered:" and says the NURSE'S part:
    "As ordered: give <drug class> - check <what> before giving, watch for <adverse effect>,
     hold and report if <finding>."

  WHY IT MATTERS FOR THE PNLE (put this thinking into "Exam traps")
    - "What should the nurse do FIRST?" is usually an independent nursing action (assess,
      position, open the airway, ensure safety), not "give a drug" or "prepare for surgery".
    - "Refer to / notify the physician" is right when the finding needs a medical decision and the
      nurse has already done what is within nursing scope. It is wrong when an independent action
      should come first, and wrong when it delays a life-saving step the nurse can take.
    - A choice that makes the nurse diagnose, prescribe, change a dose or do a medical procedure
      is out of scope, so it is wrong.
    - Delegation: the RN does assessment, teaching, evaluation and unstable patients; stable,
      routine, unchanging tasks can go to nursing aides/midwives within their scope.

PART 3. NOTE STRUCTURES (THE MERGE CHECKER ENFORCES THESE HEADINGS AND ORDER)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Markdown allowed in "body": "## " headings, paragraphs, "- " bullets, "1. " numbered lists,
**bold**, *italic*, [[Wikilinks]] to exact existing note titles. No tables, HTML, images,
code, "#" or "###". Body length 300 to 12,000 characters.

A) DISEASE / CONDITION / CLINICAL PROBLEM NOTE  (exactly these 15 headings, this order)
## What it is
   1-3 sentences: definition, who gets it, why it matters on the PNLE.
## Causative agent
   Organism (genus/species and type) or cause/risk factors if not infectious. For communicable
   diseases: mode of transmission, incubation period, period of communicability, vector or
   reservoir found in the Philippines. Nursing concept with no agent: "Not applicable" + risk factors.
## Main problem
   The core pathophysiology in 1-3 sentences (the "why" behind the signs).
## Initial manifestations
   The early signs the nurse sees first. "(pathognomonic)" only if a source says so.
## Other manifestations
   Later, severe, complicating and danger signs that need referral.
## Laboratory and diagnostic data
   Test, expected abnormal result, screening vs confirmatory. Normal ranges only if a source gives them.
## Medical management
   What the physician does (PART 2). Short bullets starting "The physician ...".
## Pharmacologic care
   For each drug CLASS used: prototype drug names (generic; Philippine National Formulary names
   when you can verify them), what it does in 1 line, then the NURSE'S responsibilities:
   before (baseline to check, e.g. apical pulse), during (how it is given safely), after
   (therapeutic effect, adverse effects, toxicity signs, when to hold and report), and teaching.
   DOSES: only if a source you opened states it, written "per source: ...; verify against current
   order/guideline". Otherwise write "dose per physician order".
## Nursing diagnoses
   2-5 NANDA-I style (problem related to cause as evidenced by signs), in priority order
   (ABC, Maslow, safety). Mark the top one "(priority)".
## Independent nursing interventions
   Numbered, in priority order. "Action - rationale". Assessment first. No drugs here.
## Collaborative nursing interventions
   Numbered. Every line starts "As ordered:" and states the nurse's part (PART 2).
## Philippine context
   DOH program (e.g. EPI, NTP/DOTS, Dengue Prevention and Control Program, Rabies Prevention and
   Control Program, IMCI, Philippine Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response), verified RA / DOH AO
   numbers and names, Filipino terms (tigdas, bulutong), local exam traps.
## Exam traps
   3-5 bullets: classic PNLE confusions and the right thinking, including at least one
   "nursing action vs doctor's action" trap and one priority trap (ABC / Maslow / safety).
## References
   2-5 numbered references you actually opened: "1. Author/Org. Title. Year or accessed date. URL"
   For a book in the owner's Drive: "1. Hinkle & Cheever. Brunner & Suddarth's ... 15th ed. Chapter <n>.
   (owner's library)". Never invent a page number, edition, URL, law number or year.
## Check against your textbook
   Keep the existing text, then add "UNVERIFIED: ..." for anything you could not confirm.

B) DRUG-CLASS NOTE (pharmacology; GEN pack "gen-pharm.json")  (exactly these 11 headings)
## What it is
## Prototype drugs
## Mechanism of action
## Indications
## Side and adverse effects
## Contraindications and cautions
## Nursing responsibilities      (before / during / after giving; what to hold and report)
## Health teaching
## Exam traps                    (include look-alike drugs, antidotes, toxicity signs)
## References
## Check against your textbook

C) NON-DISEASE NOTE (programs, laws, concepts, procedures, growth and development, communication)
   Use only the sections that fit, but always: "## What it is", "## Philippine context",
   "## Exam traps", "## References", "## Check against your textbook".
   Procedure notes: add "## Nurse's role before / during / after" and keep the doctor's part
   under "## Physician's role" if a doctor performs it.

PLAIN-WORDS RULE: short bullets, exam-ready, own words. Never copy textbook or reviewer
paragraphs; short facts and official names are fine.

PART 5. CROSS-CHECK (EVERY NOTE, EVERY TIME)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
After writing a note, check it a second time, as a strict examiner, before sending it:
  1. Check every key fact in Causative agent, Laboratory data, Pharmacologic care,
     Philippine context against at least TWO independent sources: one textbook from
     "12 Reference shelf" AND one official online source (PART 4). A review book does not count
     as one of the two.
  2. Check that nothing a doctor does sits under a nursing heading, and that every collaborative
     line starts "As ordered:".
  3. Check priorities: assessment before action, ABC, Maslow, safety, least invasive first.
  4. Anything the two sources do not both support: fix it, or move it to "Check against your
     textbook" as UNVERIFIED.
  5. Record the result in the op's "crosscheck" field (PART 7):
       "crosscheck": { "sources": ["Brunner & Suddarth 15e ch 28", "DOH dengue CPG 2024 URL"],
                       "verdict": "AGREE" | "FIXED" | "DOUBT",
                       "doubts": ["what is still uncertain"] }
     AGREE = both sources support the note. FIXED = you corrected something (list it in NOTES as
     "CORRECTED: old -> new (source)"). DOUBT = something stays uncertain (list it in "doubts").
Claude's checker flags every missing, incomplete or DOUBT cross-check for a human look.
